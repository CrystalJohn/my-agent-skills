# Pattern selection for learning products

These are authored design proposals informed by [sources](sources.md), not a validated universal taxonomy. Source references support the stated premise, not every proposed UI. Read only patterns relevant to the current job.

## Learning and navigation patterns

|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 Resume hub | Returning learner needs a next action: current objective, last meaningful checkpoint, relevant review, optional browse. | A single course can open directly at its checkpoint; no mandatory dashboard. | A wall of metrics without a next action. Resolve missing/withdrawn checkpoint and first use. | E1, E8; adaptation is inference. |

| P09 Progress / learning history | Learner or educator needs a longer view: objective coverage, evidence dates, practice history, next review. | Simple checklist for a short course; separate educator views and permissions. | “Mastered” based on attendance or a single answer. ## Cross-cutting patterns

**P11 Empty, locked, unavailable, and recovery.** First use can offer a start action; no results should offer a query/filter change; a locked activity should explain a real prerequisite; missing content and connectivity failure need their own recovery path. Do not render all four as “No lessons.” Preserve known successful work and show what is unsaved. Carbon documents contextual empty-state guidance [E8]; application error semantics remain the product's responsibility.

**P12 Interruption and confirmation.** Use a modal for a focused decision that genuinely interrupts the current task, not as the default container for a lesson. If modal: focus enters, stays inside while open, Escape closes where appropriate to the implemented pattern, and closing returns focus logically; background is inert [E10]. A full page fits a long task with independent history/resume. A nonmodal panel fits reference material that must remain usable beside the activity. Declare unsaved-work behavior.

**P13 Reward and optional social layer.** Tie a reward to an explicitly defined action; explain how it is earned and whether repeat credit exists. Consider personal progress or cooperative goals before defaulting to public competition. Competition is not universally bad: E4 reports potentially beneficial combinations with collaboration, while E5 reports adverse cases. Decide from learner context, age, stakes, fairness, and observed behavior. Never add streak loss, public ranking, life systems, monetization, or reward removal silently. Economy changes are proposals until accepted within the task scope.

**P14 Author/teacher management.** Use a table when users must compare, filter, or act on structured records [E11]. Keep this job distinct from the learner's practice surface. Prefer a simple list for a small set without comparison needs. Specify bulk selection scope, permissions, partial failure, draft/published status, and content revision impact. Source guidance does not make a table a spreadsheet replacement.

## Resolve common decisions

- **List versus map:** choose by navigation meaning, screen reader path, mobile density, and task success; aesthetic preference alone does not establish learning benefit.
- **One item versus integrated workspace:** isolated retrieval may fit one item; source comparison, coding, or simulations may require persistent context. GOV.UK question pages are transactional guidance, not evidence all learning must be single-question [E12].
- **Feedback now versus later:** practice often benefits from actionable feedback [E3]; exam integrity and pedagogical design determine when answers may be revealed. Do not infer universal immediate correctness feedback.
- **Retry versus restart:** state the learning reason and approved stakes. Technical failure must not be treated as evidence of an incorrect answer. Reconcile uncertain commits before retrying.
- **Simplify versus add a screen:** remove duplicated decisions first. E7 explicitly recommends simplifying before introducing a multi-task overview; its transfer to learning is a design inference.
